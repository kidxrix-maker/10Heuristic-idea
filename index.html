Jakob Nielsen's 10 Usability Heuristics

Ten everyday examples, one idea each.

01
Visibility of System Status

Gmail / Google Photos Trash shows “Will be permanently deleted in 30 days” next to deleted items.

Why it fits: The system clearly tells users the current status of their files and what will happen next before it happens.

02
Match Between System and the Real World

Bathroom sink hot and cold taps use red and blue, matching what people naturally expect.

Why it fits: It uses familiar real-world conventions instead of requiring users to learn an arbitrary system.

03
User Control and Freedom

Instagram DM / KakaoTalk “Unsend” allows users to take back a message after accidentally sending it.

Why it fits: It provides an “emergency exit” when users make a mistake.

04
Consistency and Standards

Traffic lights use red, yellow, and green consistently.

Why it fits: Users do not need to relearn the meaning of the signals because the same colors have the same meaning everywhere.

05
Error Prevention

ATMs often require users to take their card before receiving cash.

Why it fits: The design prevents users from making a common mistake—forgetting their card—by controlling the order of actions.

06
Recognition Rather Than Recall

Restaurant menus with photos show users what each dish looks like.

Why it fits: Users can recognize their options visually instead of having to remember or imagine what each dish is.

07
Flexibility and Efficiency of Use

Subway card readers allow experienced commuters to quickly tap their card while remaining simple enough for first-time users.

Why it fits: The same system supports both experienced and inexperienced users.

08
Aesthetic and Minimalist Design

Google's homepage contains only the essential elements: a logo, a search bar, and a few buttons.

Why it fits: Nothing unnecessary competes for the user's attention.

09
Help Users Recognize, Diagnose, and Recover from Errors

Naver Map automatically calculates a new route when a user misses a turn.

Why it fits: Instead of simply telling the user that they made a mistake, the system recognizes the problem and immediately provides a solution.

10
Help and Documentation

A “?” icon next to a password field can show password requirements when the user needs them.

Why it fits: Help is available exactly where and when it is needed, without forcing users to search through a separate help page.
